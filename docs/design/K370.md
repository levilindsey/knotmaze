# K370: input latency (stage 1: measure, then the levers that pay)

Ledger: K370. One stage, opus, 2 h cap. Starts after K573 (the phone gap
search fix) merges, because K573 changes the phone's frame time and so
its latency.

## Where it stands

A drag's latency is measured from a pointer event to the first frame
whose grabbed point covers 90% of where it settles for that step (K7
stage 4's definition; see "Where it departs from the brief" in
`docs/design/K7_worker.md`). The target is a p50 of 50 ms or less.

| | worker (default) | in-thread (`phw=0`) |
|---|---|---|
| desktop, daily | 105 ms | 285 ms |
| desktop, knot | 70 ms | |
| Pixel 11, daily touch (12 steps) | p50 231, p95 392; first motion 173 | p50 872 |

Sources: K7 stage 4's latency step (`k7_latency.mjs`, under the perf
lock), and stage 5's phone table and handoff in `K7_worker.md`. Stage 5's
handoff also lists the phone harness: `k7_latency.mjs` with `--phone`,
`phone_lib.mjs`, `table_s5.mjs`.

## Steps

1. **Rebuild the harness on master, then measure where the time goes.**
   - Port the harness to today's master if it needs it.
   - Add per-stage timestamps along the path:
     - the event's queue (`timeStamp` to the handler);
     - main's post to the worker;
     - the substeps until the grabbed point settles;
     - the snapshot's transfer;
     - the interpolation wait (about one frame, D);
     - the frame itself (render and present).
   - Measure on the desktop (daily and knot mode) and on the Pixel (the
     daily), both hosts, enough steps for a p50 and a p95.
2. **Try the levers the numbers support.** Inferred so far:
   - draw the newest snapshot without interpolation while a thread is
     grabbed (saves about D, 11 ms at 56 fps);
   - let the worker run more substeps per frame while dragging (on the
     daily it runs about 1.6, at 10.7 ms each);
   - on the phone, the worker's 15-37 ms substeps. Stage 5 inferred it
     sometimes lands on a slower core.

   Look also at the input side: `pointerrawupdate`, coalesced events, and
   whether a move waits for a rAF before it is posted.

   A/B each lever, and keep only the ones that pay without new jitter.
3. **Report a table** before and after, per stage and per case. Name
   what's left between the result and the 50 ms target, and what it
   would take.

### Verdicts

- **A, no interpolation while grabbed: OFF** (the option stays). It
  removes the daily's interpolation penalty (mean -9 ms) but doubles
  the jitter (0.18 to 0.42 on the daily, 0.13 to 0.20 on the knot) and
  delays first motion on the daily (p50 66 to 90, not attributed). It
  does nothing on the knot.
- **B, raw input: ON** (`RAW_DRAG_DEFAULT = true`, `7a36688`).
  - It cuts the daily's mean by 7 ms (4-14 in three paired runs), with
    p95 at or under B off's in each pair and the same jitter.
  - It costs the knot nothing in latency.
  - The knot's jitter was higher with B in the final pass but not in
    the lever pass. That is a residual to re-measure, not a shown
    regression.
  - The 4 ms throttle keeps a 1000 Hz mouse from posting a target
    every millisecond, and the worker from packing a snapshot as often.
  - On the phone it is neutral.
- **More substeps while dragging: not built.** The worker already runs
  them back to back while a step responds, and in-thread main already
  steps 12 a frame. More substeps can only mean cheaper ones.
- **The phone's 15-37 ms substeps: not fixed, not attributed.** They
  don't come from a little core: the core check above found the worker
  on the 4.11 GHz core. The candidates and the next check are listed
  there. These substeps and the 53 ms queue dominate the phone's
  latency (work is 85-95 ms of 181-195).
- **Coalesced events: subsumed by B.** The grab chases the newest
  target, so the in-between samples add nothing; B gets the newest one
  sooner.
- **The rAF wait:** about 6 ms on the worker host (pointerrawupdate
  runs 5.8-8.6 ms before the aligned pointermove, p50), which B
  removes. In-thread, posting sooner can't help: the input waits for
  main's 130-162 ms frame either way.

### The gap to 50 ms

On the desktop's worker host the daily's drag latency averages about
110 ms with B (102-115 in three runs; 118 with B off), the knot's about
92 (91-95). The daily's stage p50s:
work 53, main's frame 22, vsync 10, display 9, queue 6, pickup 6,
transfer 1. What closing each part would take:

1. **Work: 4 substeps, 42-54 ms.** Fewer substeps per step, or cheaper
   ones:
   - a stiffer grab. The chase rate (0.6 per constraint iteration)
     decides how many substeps reach 90%. That is a feel change, so it
     is Levi's call; an A/B of the rate (0.8, 1.0) would show both the
     substeps saved and how it feels. At 2 substeps the daily's work
     would be about 26 ms.
   - cheaper substeps. The WASM collision narrow-phase (40% of drag CPU
     at the 2026-10-10 audit, an estimated 2-4x on that slice) gives
     about 1.3-1.4x overall. Finding why the worker's substeps cost
     20-35% more than in-thread ones on the same page may give as much
     again.
   Neither alone reaches 50 ms: 4 x 13 ms / 1.4 is still 37 ms of work.
2. **Main's drag frame: 19-24 ms of JS.** It halves the frame rate,
   which costs the frame, the vsync and the rAF wait, about 40 ms
   against about 20 at 60 fps. Closing it means main's frame under about
   12 ms while dragging.

   A sampling profile of main during a daily drag (`prof-daily` on
   `builds/map`, the final build with source maps; 468 frames, taken
   after the phone check ended) shows where main's time goes:
   - `RibbonRenderer.update`: 63% of main's sampled time;
   - the crossing gap slots (`computeGapSlot`): 28% inclusive;
   - the spline evaluation (`spline_generator.ts`): 22%;
   - `tickPaint`'s palette and fillet refresh: 17%;
   - the mesh writes (`ribbon_mesh.update`): 8% self.

   So a 12 ms drag frame means a cheaper per-frame ribbon rebuild,
   starting with the gap slots and the spline resampling. On the knot
   (`prof-knot`), main's frame costs 19.2 ms at p50.
3. **The rest, about 15 ms**: queue 6 (the event waits for main's
   current frame), pickup 6 (the worker finishes the substep it is in,
   half a substep on average: a message cannot preempt one), transfer 1
   and display 9 (the rAF wait). B takes about 6 ms of this.

So 50 ms at p50 takes all three: a 2-substep grab (or substeps 2.5x
cheaper), a 60 fps main frame while dragging, and B. The in-thread host
(240-300 ms) is a separate problem: real-time stepping spirals to 12
substeps a frame while dragging (K279's WAKE COST), so its latency is two
of those frames. It is the fallback, and only cheaper substeps help it.
