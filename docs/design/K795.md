## Background

The beat rider (K114, `clk=beat`) has three params:
- `beatTempo` (`btp`, 100);
- `beatLength` (`btl`, 2);
- `beatCap` (`btc`, 4).

They sit near lines 808-813 and 1143-1146 of `src/ui/url_state.ts`, with
their readers near line 1946 and `beatSettingsOf` near line 165. The
clock itself is `clock` (`clk`: turn, realtime or beat; `CLOCK_MODES` near
line 118).

The beat stage hit its cap before its item 8: the panel rows, the HUD
tempo row and the share tag (`docs/design/K114_beat.md`, Handoff and
Remainder). So the beat rider can be reached only by a link.

The panel (`src/ui/tweakpane_panel.ts`) builds rows with its `row(...)`
helper (near line 744). Follow the pattern of the fire clock: its rows
are near line 1559, and their visibility near line 2957.

## This stage, in order

1. **The beat rider's rows.**
   - A clock row (`clk`: turn, realtime, beat) beside the chase's rows, if
     none exists. Show it wherever a mode reads `clk`; check which modes
     do.
   - Rows for tempo, length and cap, shown only under `clk=beat`.
     - Name them as a player would ("Beat tempo (bpm)", say).
     - Tooltips say what each does in play. Take that from the beat's lore
       (find it: `gameplay-minotaur.md` or the K114 brief).
   - A LIVE_EFFECTS entry (`undo_stack.ts`, near line 135) or a restart,
     the same as the fire clock's rows.
   - For the daily, follow the fire clock's rows (exempt or not;
     `daily_link.ts`).
   - The HUD tempo row and the share tag (the rest of K114's item 8) are
     not this stage. List them under Ledger items if they are still
     missing.
2. **Every param has a row, or a reason.**
   - A test in `test/ui/` walks `PARAM_DEFAULTS`. It fails for a key that
     has neither a panel row nor an entry in an explicit exemption list
     with a one-line reason: `phw`, read once at load; a link-only debug
     or probe flag.
   - Pick the cheapest way for the test to know which keys have rows. A
     source scan of the `row(...)` calls is acceptable, and so is a list
     the panel builder exports. Don't build Tweakpane in Node for it.
   - Give a row to every key that deserves one and has none.
3. **Audit the hard-coded tunables of recent features**, and add params
   for the ones that deserve them.
   - **Scope:** the features since K80: the minotaur, K114 (the fire, the
     cut, the walker, the beat), K137 (fragile runs, par, lures and
     snares), K138 stages 1-3 (turnstiles, one-way runs and the rest),
     and K139 (see `LEDGER.md`). Leave the locks to K138.
   - **For each candidate:** file:line, value, what it changes in play, a
     proposed range and step, and a verdict: a param, not worth one (and
     why), or a call for Levi.
   - **"Deserve":** someone tuning a mode would reach for it, such as
     speeds, counts, densities, durations, and the sizes players see.
     Internal epsilons, solver constants and render plumbing don't.
   - **Add params for the top ones**, best first, as many as fit after
     items 1 and 2. Each:
     - has today's constant as its default, so every maze and every pin
       stays bit for bit (the golden pins must not move);
     - is appended to `CODEC_PARAMS` (`CODEC_VERSION` stays), with
       `PARAM_RANGES`, an override key, `url_codec.test.ts`'s defaults
       table and OVERRIDE_KEYS, and the table pin in
       `param_codec.test.ts`;
     - gets a panel row with a tooltip, shown only where it applies;
     - gets a LIVE_EFFECTS entry or a restart;
     - gets a daily decision: a gameplay param changes the daily's design,
       so it is not exempt; a viewer preference is exempt;
     - gets lore in `gameplay-links.md` (the codec table) and
       `gameplay-panel.md`.
   - The audit table goes in the Handoff. The params you don't add are
     the next stage's list.
