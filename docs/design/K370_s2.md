# K370 stage 2: the worker's substep premium (brief)

Ledger: K370. Stage 1's record and tables: `docs/design/K370.md`'s
Handoff. The pickup sequence (item 17) takes this stage as: brief
first, the desktop half here, the phone half in the Wave 5 window.

## The question

Stage 1 measured the same substep costing more in the worker than
in-thread on the same page and machine — desktop daily 13.1-13.4 ms
against 10.8, knot 10.3-11.8 against 8.7 (20-35%); phone 22.8 against
8.1 (2.1-2.9x, on the fastest core, not a little-core artifact).
Untested candidates from stage 1: the worker isolate's JIT tier or
GC; contention with main's render; (phone only) the cpu6 governor's
actual clock. Closing even the desktop premium is worth ~1.2-1.35x on
the work stage (42-54 ms of the daily's ~110 ms drag latency), which
stage 1's gap-to-50 table prices.

## The desktop half (this stage)

1. Re-measure on today's master with stage 1's harness
   (`.claude/probes/K370/k370_latency.mjs`, the perf lock held):
   daily + knot, worker vs in-thread, substeps × ms each, means over
   at least two runs per case (stage 1: compare means, the latency is
   bimodal). Note what R3/R4 and everything since stage 1 moved.
2. Attribute the premium, cheapest discriminating checks first:
   - a worker drag with main's rendering paused (the harness's
     continuous loop off / a covered canvas): if the premium survives,
     contention is out;
   - CDP CPU profiles of the worker thread vs main stepping the same
     scene (`k370_profile.mjs` profiles main; extend its target
     picker to the worker): same functions, different shares → JIT/GC
     shape; a GC share → allocation in the step;
   - substep cost vs step count into a drag (tier-up curve): a
     premium that decays over the first seconds is JIT tiering.
3. Whatever the attribution supports, build the smallest lever and
   A/B it with the harness (latency means + jitter, both hosts, plus
   the rest A/B: no pixels change at rest). Physics stays
   deterministic: both hosts in lockstep, bit for bit
   (`physics_host.test.ts`, `byte_identity.test.ts`).

## Handoff — desktop half (2026-10-21, master 6bf4a6ad)

Re-measured with stage 1's harness (`.claude/probes/K370/s2_batch.txt`,
8 runs, perf lock held, build `builds/cur` from 6bf4a6ad; data
`s2.jsonl`, table via `table_k370.mjs`). Means of two runs each:

| case | latency mean | work | substeps × ms | in-thread ms/sub |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| daily, worker | 142.0 | 78 | 7 × 11.35 | 10.8 |
| knot, worker | 67.1 | 35 | 4 × 8.7 | 8.4 |
| daily, in-thread | 307.7 | — | 12 × 10.8 | — |
| knot, in-thread | 233.7 | — | 12 × 8.4 | — |

**The premium is gone on the desktop.** Stage 1's worker substep cost
20-35% more than in-thread on the same page; today it is +4% (daily)
and +4% (knot) — inside run-to-run noise. Whatever stage 1 measured
(JIT tier in a fresh isolate is the remaining plausible candidate) does
not reproduce on the current build, so there is no lever to build:
closing step 3 with no change. The knot's drag latency nearly halved
since stage 1 (mean 92.7 → 67.1; its frame interval is 16.7 ms now,
against 32.5 then).

Two confounds, recorded honestly:

- The daily is a different maze (Daily #10, not stage 1's): it needs
  7 substeps to cover 90% of a step, against stage 1's 4, which is
  most of its worse latency (work 78 vs ~55). Substep COUNT is the
  grab-chase geometry (K610/K316's feel call), not this stage's lever.
- The daily's frame interval measured 66.7 ms in both worker runs
  while the knot held 16.7, with similar frame JS (~23 ms). Observed,
  not attributed: either the daily scene's drag frame genuinely misses
  two vsyncs on this machine now, or the batch's early runs overlapped
  this session's own builds. K611 (main's drag frame under 12 ms)
  owns the follow-up either way.
