## Steps

1. **The part argument.** `lintGlyph` takes the taper part as an
   argument, defaulting to `TAPER_PART_LU`, and every caller that knows
   its part passes it. tp60 then lints without a copy. A test lints a
   glyph at both parts.
2. **Injected cases.** A probe-only flag (a `#glyphs2` hash option, like
   `notaper`, `aland` and `tp60`) that adds a bulge of a given size to
   the taper outline, and another that widens a crotch to a given length
   past R. The flags change nothing when absent: the golden stays
   byte-identical and no link changes.
3. **Stills.** Render the a (and `ai`) with injected bulges across the
   bracket (for example 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.015, 0.022, 0.03 LU) and
   crotches near the bound (for example 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0 LU), at
   the desk scale (1600x1000, dsf 3, xh 2 and 3, seeds 7 and 9) and the
   phone scale (411x753, dsf 2, seed 7). Make one labelled contact sheet
   per bound.
4. **Calibrate.** Each bound becomes the largest value that still looks
   clean at the phone scale and the desk scale, whichever is tighter.
   Write down the evidence (the sheet, the cell where it first shows) in
   the Handoff and in the constant's comment. If 0.01 and 1.2 hold, keep
   them and say so.
5. **Report the K718 cases** against the calibrated bounds: where the J
   bite and the waist spikes fall. Report only; Levi decides.
6. **Tests and build.** Update `test/core/glyph_lint.test.ts` (and
   `glyph_taper.test.ts` if it pins a bound). `npm test` and
   `npm run build` pass, and the golden stays byte-identical.
