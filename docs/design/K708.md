2. **The sword, passive players.** The scripted players do not go for the sword: it is taken only when their walk crosses its spot, in 13.0-23.3% of real-time games and 13.0-15.3% of turn-clock games, and it banished or slew in 0.0-1.4% of games (turn clock: 0.0%). Mean catches a game fall by 0.04-0.13 in real time and 0.00-0.05 on the turn clock; the 3+ share falls by 0.0-2.3 points in real time and about 0.5 points on the turn clock. `timed` and `oneuse` differ by at most 0.02 catches and 0.3 points in real time, and not at all on the turn clock.
3. **The sword, a player who goes for it (turn clock only).** `seeker` (my addition: walks the fewest runs to the sword's spot, then chases the fleeing minotaur by the telegraph) takes the sword in every game, before any catch, and touches the minotaur (banish or slay) in 0.5% of games at the default `swordMoves` 8 (4 of 864). The touch rate climbs with `swordMoves`: 0.1% at 4, 0.5% at 8, 4.2% at 12, 19.3% at 20. ### What the tables suggest (inferred; decisions for Levi)

- **Lives 3: keep.** On real time it is a real constraint for blind play (about a third of games reach 3 catches; 2 hearts would end 47-48%) and mild for players who flee (3.5-11%). On the turn clock it is generous: only blind play reaches 3 catches (8.4%), a telegraph reader never; 2 hearts is the first setting that bites there (16.6% blind, 3.8% fleer, 0.1% aware). One value serves both clocks, so 3 is a sensible middle.
- **`swordMoves` 8 makes the sword a breather on the turn clock, not a weapon.** Even the best-case seeker touches the minotaur in 0.5% of games, and the sword changes a passive player's catches by at most 0.13 a game. Mechanism, from the K137 s3b flee rule in the code (the flee hop takes the move whose far end is farthest from the player in runs, and holds at a vertex when every move would close in): it runs at the player's speed, so a same-speed chaser reaches it only when it is cornered. That fits the numbers (touches only from near pickups, the rate climbing with the flee's length) but no run changed the flee rule, so it is a reading, not a test. If the sword should be able to slay there, `swordMoves` alone will not do it (12 gives 4.2%, the range's top 20 gives 19.3%); the flee rule or its pace is the lever.
- **Real time is open.** The real-time sim has no seeker kind, so the sword's best case there is unmeasured. From the s3b lore the real-time flee runs at the wander pace (half the player's), so a real-time seeker may do much better than the turn clock's; this run does not say.
- **`timed` vs `oneuse`:** the counts cannot tell them apart at these touch rates (they differ only after a touch); choose on feel.

It already respawns on a catch and counts catches; the cap is 900 s. Reps vary the player's think-time stream (the chase's seed is the design seed).
- **Players:** real time `greedy`, `fleer`, `fleerq` (K324's: blind explorer; greedy until hunted, then flee; the same with queued taps). Turn clock `greedy` and `fleer` (the same rules on runs), plus two of mine: `aware` (greedy that reads the telegraph and avoids a run that would be a catch: K80's "explorer") and `seeker` (above; the sword's best case; as `aware` once the sword is spent or slain, and with the sword off it IS `aware`).
- **Sword:** laid where `main.ts` `laySword` lays it (`swordSpot(g, designSeed, avoid)`: `{hops: 1}` for the hunter and the stalker, the guard's territory in thread units for the guard), set with `setSword`, and taken, fled, banished or slain inside the game modules. - **Controls:** the sword-off real-time rows reproduce K324's `results_s3.md` exactly (greedy 2.61 / 2.49 / 2.72, fleer 0.72 / 0.78 / 0.56, fleerq 0.43 / 0.86 / 0.50 catches a game for hunter / guard / stalker). A laid but untaken sword leaves a game identical (checked on seed 1). The turn rerun that added the pickup diagnostics matches the earlier rows exactly (0 of 10368 differ outside the three new fields).
- **Lives are modelled OFF.** The page ends the level on the Lth catch (hearts refilled, a new sword laid, the tallies carried). These sims play a catch as a plain respawn and run to the exit or the cap, so "3+ catches" is the share of games that would run out of 3 hearts at least once (exact, since the first three catches play out the same either way). The number of run-outs a game and what follows a restart are not modelled.
