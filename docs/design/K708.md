### What the tables suggest (inferred; decisions for Levi)

- **Lives 3: keep.** On real time it is a real constraint for blind play (about a third of games reach 3 catches; 2 hearts would end 47-48%) and mild for players who flee (3.5-11%). On the turn clock it is generous: only blind play reaches 3 catches (8.4%), a telegraph reader never; 2 hearts is the first setting that bites there (16.6% blind, 3.8% fleer, 0.1% aware). One value serves both clocks, so 3 is a sensible middle.
- **`swordMoves` 8 makes the sword a breather on the turn clock, not a weapon.** Even the best-case seeker touches the minotaur in 0.5% of games, and the sword changes a passive player's catches by at most 0.13 a game. Mechanism, from the K137 s3b flee rule in the code (the flee hop takes the move whose far end is farthest from the player in runs, and holds at a vertex when every move would close in): it runs at the player's speed, so a same-speed chaser reaches it only when it is cornered. That fits the numbers (touches only from near pickups, the rate climbing with the flee's length) but no run changed the flee rule, so it is a reading, not a test. If the sword should be able to slay there, `swordMoves` alone will not do it (12 gives 4.2%, the range's top 20 gives 19.3%); the flee rule or its pace is the lever.
- **Real time is open.** The real-time sim has no seeker kind, so the sword's best case there is unmeasured. From the s3b lore the real-time flee runs at the wander pace (half the player's), so a real-time seeker may do much better than the turn clock's; this run does not say.
- **`timed` vs `oneuse`:** the counts cannot tell them apart at these touch rates (they differ only after a touch); choose on feel.

### K783: lures and snares on the turn clock

Turn clock at the shipped hunter defaults, sword off, no extra monsters (only the minotaur hears a lure or springs a snare; the sleeper's lure-wake is unmeasured). A stock is per game, as on the page it refills on a level restart, not on a catch, and this sim has no restarts. Stocks 0 to 3 for each tool, crossed (the `lures` param allows 0..5; the brief asked 0..3). Players greedy and fleer, moving exactly as `turn_run.ts` moves them. How they use the tools (both free in time, judged before each move, the lure first):

- Lure: while the minotaur hunts and stands within 3 runs of the player, pluck the run whose lure target (`lureTarget` for the minotaur's vertex, range 6) is farthest from the player in runs, among targets farther from the player than the minotaur stands; ties to the lowest run id. No such run, no lure spent. Re-judged every turn.
- Snare: when the telegraph's committed move ends on the player's vertex, lay the snare on that move's arrival run, so the minotaur's turn is cut at the run's near end and it stands 3 turns.

Reading: the lure does the work. On the greedy line each lure buys less than the last (0.69 -> 0.38 -> 0.20 -> 0.14; the third buys 0.06), and the fleer's catches are already low (0.20 -> 0.08 at 3). Suggested stock: **lures 2, snares 1**. Two lures take greedy catches from 0.69 to 0.20 a game and the 3+ share from 8.4% to about 2%; the third lure buys 0.06 more. One snare keeps the arrival block for its small gain; the second and third buy 0.01 or less. The real-time tools (`MinotaurChase.lure` / `setSnare`, added after this item was filed) are not simulated here.

### K728: a sword-seeking player in real time

`seeker` added to `rt/rt_sim.ts`: it beelines for the sword's spot (the run whose far end has the fewest thread distance to the spot, whole graph), takes it, then runs the fleeing minotaur down (at each vertex, the run whose far end is nearest the minotaur's spot, restricted distance); spent or done, it plays greedy to the exit, and with no sword laid it is greedy. It never flees and thinks at the greedy pace (0.25-0.75 s), so its catches price the best case. Sweep `s728`: hunter, guard and stalker at hunt pace 0.9, detect 15, player pace 10, stops and pull as `s708`; sword timed and oneuse at `swordMoves` 8. ## K1056: lures and snares in real time (2026-10-29)

Run F of `docs/design/K1053_muse_batch2.md`: the K783 stocks on the real-time chase, which run A measured on the turn clock only. No `src/` change and no default change; the harness changes live in `.claude/probes/K708/` (untracked): changed `rt/rt_sim.ts` (the tool policies and tool counts), `rt/rt_sweeps.ts` (`s1056`), `rt/rt_run.ts` (stock and spent fields on each row) and `rt/rt_lib.ts` (the lure exports); new `rt/control1056.ts` (the step-1 control), `rt/check_tools1056.ts` (the hand check), `rt/trace_lure1056.ts` and `summarize_runF.mjs`. Both tools go through the game's own calls, made as `src/main.ts` makes them (`MinotaurChase.lure(edge, lureRangeOf(params.lureRange))` at the shipped range, and `MinotaurChase.setSnare(edge)`); no lure, snare or catch rule is copied. The counts are deterministic. Mazes: the same 288 H1 mazes x 3 reps = 864 games a cell as the tables above.

Settings: the shipped real-time hunter — pace 10, wander pace 5 (0.5 x), hunt pace 9 (0.9 x), jitter 0.2, pauses 0.3 at 0.5-2 s, detect 15 / lose 25, catch 1, dead-end bias 0.4, roam 30, wake 2, roam pull 0.5, junction stops 0.2-0.6 s (x0.5 hunting), model hunter; sword off, no extra monsters. The harness cannot take these from `PARAM_DEFAULTS` directly (its params are `ChaseParams`-shaped), so it takes them from `DEFAULT_CHASE_PARAMS`, which `PARAM_DEFAULTS` itself reads — the values are the same by construction (`chaseParamsOf` resolves the panel's pace fractions to 5 and 9 at pace 10). Two shipped values are not `ChaseParams` at all and the harness takes them from their constants: lure range 6 runs (`lureRange` = `LURE_RANGE`) and snare hold 3 s (`chase.snareHoldS` = `snareHoldOf(params.snareHold)`, `SNARE_HOLD` 3). Stocks are per game and do not refill on a catch.

### The policies

Judged only at the player's decision points — standing at a vertex after the think time, about to tap (the fleerq queued tap is not in this sweep) — the lure first, then the snare, both free in time. The player's run is chosen after the tools, under the existing greedy / fleer rules (so the fleer reads hunting after a lure, as the turn fleer does). Distances are in runs (`SimGraph.hop`, the turn sim's `hopd`); the minotaur's vertex is where it stands, or the vertex it heads to mid-run (the vertex `chase.lure` itself reads); the player's vertex is where it stands.

- Lure: while the minotaur hunts within 3 runs of the player, pluck the run whose lure destination (`lureTarget` for the minotaur's vertex, range 6) lies farthest from the player in runs, among those farther from the player than the minotaur; ties to the lowest run id; with none, spend nothing.
- Snare: while it hunts, take its route to the player (`chase.plan(8)`, the telegraph's own read): if a step of that route that it has not entered yet ends on the player's vertex, lay the snare on that step's run. (The route's lead run is already entered, so it never qualifies.) A snare springs when the minotaur steps onto its run and holds it for `snareHoldS`, 3 s.

Hand check (`rt/check_tools1056.ts`, one game, seed 2 rep 0, stocks 3 / 3): a spent lure is heard and turns the minotaur — `t=13.95 LURE run 36 -> vertex 48 heard 1 mode hunt->wander dest 131->48` (and twice more) — and a laid snare springs and holds it: laid at `t=203.03` (run 221) and `t=203.54` (run 279), sprung at `t=204.72` with `left 360 ticks` (3 s at 120 Hz), released at `t=207.72` with the minotaur standing at the same vertex. The check asserts the roughly 360-tick hold.

Reading: the real-time tools trade places with the turn clock's. The snare does the work here: each snare at lures 0 buys the greedy player 0.11, then 0.19, then 0.17 catches (2.61 -> 2.14 over the stock), and the first two buy the fleer 0.09 and 0.07 (0.72 -> 0.56, the third a tie at 0.57) — a held minotaur stands 3 real seconds while the player crosses several runs, where the turn clock's 3-turn hold only cut the one arrival it was laid on. Combined cells add nothing over their snare stock alone past that (at (3, 3): greedy 2.27 against 2.14 snares-only, fleer 0.84 against 0.57).

Suggested real-time stock: **lures 0, snares 3**, beside the shipped 2 and 1. No lures: they save the blind player at most 0.02 a game and cost the fleer up to 0.29, at every snare stock. Snares to the stock's top (3 is `SNARES_MAX`): the third still buys the greedy player 0.17 catches and 2.6 points of 3+ share, and the fleer ties its best row there. One stock pair serves both clocks, and the turn clock's table above suggested the opposite (2 lures, 1 snare), so which stock the shared defaults should carry is Levi's call.
