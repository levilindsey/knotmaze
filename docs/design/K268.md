## Stage 1: the ribbon's shaped cut (brief, 2026-10-14 16:10)

### The work

1. **Design first.** Before writing code, append a short "Stage 1 design"
   section to this doc and commit it. It covers:
   - the profile's texel layout, with the flag in texel 8.w;
   - how a piece's ends encode as up to two ramps;
   - the signature of the shared fade-and-look function;
   - the classifier's thresholds;
   - your calls on the design's open choices: draw order (accept it
     within the fade, or keep the silhouette total above vis 0.5 and
     fade only the casing), and anything else you settle.
2. **The row-partial gap upload** (2b's residual): only changed rows
   upload. Measure the upload before and after (`lastPaintUpload`) on a
   move whose fade crosses slots.
3. **The classifier.** On each view change, sort every slot into one of
   three:
   - hidden: rejected, as 2b does;
   - solid: plain, as 2b does;
   - shaped: the flag is set.
4. **The profile and the shaped cut**, in the explore ribbon program.
   - The CPU writes each shaped slot's vis profile in over-chain arc.
   - The shader evaluates the explore program's own fade-and-look
     function at the projection, arc = polyStart + (bk + bt) *
     polyStep. Put that function in `explore_glsl.ts` as shared GLSL, so
     the cut and the thread can't disagree.
   - It covers a stub's fade and end, an arm's growth front, and all six
     end looks.
5. **The over thread's own tip** (the design's dead-end case). A stub
   that fades short of its tip at a crossing leaves no ghost notch. A
   stub that is solid to its tip stays right.
6. **The CPU mirror** in `paint_math.ts`, which the proof reads.
