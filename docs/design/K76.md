# K76 brief: a thread-reveal exploration mode

Levi, 2026-10-12 (verbatim):

> "Also, similar to fog of war mode, I'd love to add another mode option.
> In this new mode, we don't render the threads of the maze until the
> player has reached an adjacent junction for them, and we then only show
> them for a certain distance away from the junction (a set distance, not
> a segment length ratio, and this distance should be a parameter). We
> should show a gradual fade-out to the end of the shown segment length
> (the fade-out distance should be another parameter). The part I'm not
> sure of yet, is that we need to somehow make it visually clear where all
> of the unvisited segment ends are, so the ends of these segments need to
> somehow look visually distinct at a glance. Maybe a background
> highlight. Maybe an in-thread highlight near the end (in which case,
> we'd actually need to handle the fade-out differently, I guess). And in
> this mode, with cameraMode set to fit, we'd only pan/zoom to fit the
> current explored segments and the visible bits of the adjacent
> segments."

## Stage 1 (this agent): design and prototypes, no merge

1. **Mechanism.** Find the right engine and write it down.
   - The likely fit is the appearance reveal, which since G2.1 runs per
     pixel. It already hides threads, gates crossings until both passes
     show, and fades. A gameplay-driven reveal would set each chain's
     visible intervals to the traveled runs, plus `revealDistance`
     along every arm of each visited junction, with a `revealFade` ramp.
   - Gaps: a hidden over thread must not cut a gap into a visible under
     thread. Work out how the G1 casing masks and the gate show times
     handle that.
   - Also cover junction webs, markers, the yarn (`pv=string`), pulses,
     hover and hit-testing: are hidden runs tappable? They shouldn't be.
   - Cover how the mode relates to fog of war: exclusive, or composable?
2. **The unvisited-end look**, Levi's open question. Prototype at least:
   - a background halo behind each frontier end;
   - an in-thread highlight near the end, with its own fade design;
   - a cap or bead at the frontier.

   Add anything better you find. Render each on the same frozen state
   (`.claude/probes/probe_winbanner.mjs` clicks a route; pin
   `renderer.paintClock`) at desktop and phone sizes, as a contact sheet
   Levi can choose from.
3. **Camera.** With `cameraMode` fit, frame the explored runs plus the
   visible bits of the adjacent arms. Fog of war's fit-to-visible-region
   path is the precedent.
4. **Params.** A mode switch, `revealDistance` and `revealFade`, with
   names, ranges, defaults and panel placement.
   - They must be APPENDED to the current codec table, with defaults that
     leave today's links unchanged (mode off).
   - K73 is adding codec v4 right now, so the implementation stage
     appends to v4.
   - The mode is not a daily playback preference, so turning it on leaves
     the daily, unless Levi wants dailies with it (ask).
5. **Cost.** Estimate per-frame GPU and CPU, and give a phone-gate plan.

## Deliverable

`docs/design/K76_reveal_mode.md`, with the contact sheet in
`.claude/probes/K76/`. Put first the recommendation and the decisions
for Levi: the end-marker look, the param names and defaults, and the
fog-of-war relationship. Prototype code stays on the worktree branch;
the implementation is stage 2, after K73 and K75 merge.

## Constraints

- Use a private vite cacheDir for the worktree's server, and leave 5173

### Calls made in this stage

- The whole-chain flag lives in KPAINT texel `chain` .x, not in KGEOM as
  section 7 says. KGEOM's G0 is full, and G0.w is the paint flag.
- VIS is not a PAINT_ITEM: `glslConstBlock` would emit a PI_ constant into
  master's program text.
- A VIS item sets no chain's paint flag (`writePaintItems` keeps a
  `painted` array), and a whole chain gets no VIS item.
- The explore program is a separate Shader on the same resources, so with
  the mode off every ribbon binds master's program. `bindGap` binds the
  explore program's uGapData too.
- The looks are GPU-only, like K111's modes. `kExploreAt` mirrors the
  visibility, not the looks.
- The probe-only model keeps the prototype's derivation from the
  controller's sets and resets on `c.restarts`. Its flaws are listed
  under stage 2c below.

### Shaped gaps (Levi, 15:16): a brief for a later stage

Levi asked for gaps that follow the over thread's drawn extent:
- no cut where the over thread is not shown yet;
- a cut shaped by a stub's partial, moving overlap;
- dead ends handled with the same care.

2b's gate is one strength per slot, vis(over) at the over pass. It is
exact when the over thread is wholly hidden or wholly solid across the
footprint (the census's 405 and 2). It is wrong when a front lies inside
the footprint: the cut is too strong where the over thread is not drawn
(a ghost cut) and too weak where it is. Written from the code; no probe
ran.

**Cases in the code:**
- **The over thread partly shown across the footprint**: a stub's fade
  (at least FADE_MIN 0.35 ctrl) or its end, an arm's growth front (it
  moves every frame of a move), and each end look's coverage in the fade.
  Dots, fray and glow change it, and the halo and beacon sprites lie
  beneath the weave, where a gap shows them.
- **The under thread partly shown**: this composes already. vis(under)
  multiplies the fragment's alpha and the gap multiplies keep.
- **Emergent crossings** (the 64-slot pool) and **self-crossings**: the
  same polyline and over ctrl as registered slots, so they get whatever
  registered slots get. **Marker discs** (one point, polyStep 0) stay
  binary with their marker unless markers fade.
- **underWindows** (the yarn's `underGapVisibility`, the fillets'
  armReach, the pluck footprints) are binary at vis 0.5. Either clip each
  window to the arc range where the over pass shows, or accept the step.
- **Protect** waives A's margin where A covers B's ribbon. Where B is
  hidden, that removes a casing line with nothing beyond it. Scale the
  waiver by vis(B) along the protect polyline.
- **The far-branch smooth-min**: when one branch is shown and the other
  is not, the blend dips toward the hidden branch. Blend the vis-weighted
  cuts instead of the distances.
- **Junction clearance** is solved against joints whether they show or
  not. Near a hidden joint the casing is slightly narrow, which is
  cosmetic. Junction fans (ids <= -2) cut a foreign over thread's
  silhouette and need the same factor, in the fan program.
- **Dead ends**:
  - A free tip's cap is a fan with the full mask, so the same factor
    serves it.
  - The over thread's own tip in or near a footprint: the polyline ends
    at the tip with a round end. 2b reads the factor at the crossing, so
    when the stub fades just short of its tip, it still cuts that round
    end around a hidden tip, a ghost notch. A stub solid to its tip is
    right.
  - The cap gate is binary at 0.98, while a shaped cut follows the fade.
    The cap and the cut must quote one coverage, so the cap should fade
    with vis at its tip. This is the strip-alpha decision again.
- **Draw order**: inside the silhouette, a partial cut depends on which
  thread the base layer drew first. G1 is order-free only because that
  cut is total, and 2b's fading factor and the intro's ramp share this.
  Either accept it within the fade, or keep the silhouette total above
  vis 0.5 and fade only the casing.

**Recommended.** Cut each under pixel by the over thread's coverage at
its nearest point on the over centerline. The search already finds that
point (segment bk, parameter bt). Every crossing slot's polyline is evenly
spaced in over-chain arc (texel 4: polyStart = overCenter - reachNeg, and
polyStep), so the over arc there is polyStart + (bk + bt) * polyStep,
with no per-point storage.

The CPU writes each shaped slot a VIS PROFILE in that arc: the piece ends
as up to two ramps, one more texel per row (texel 8.w is free for a
flag). The shader then evaluates the same fade-and-look function that
the explore program draws the thread with, as shared GLSL, so the cut and
the thread cannot disagree.

On each view change the CPU sorts every slot into one of three:
- hidden across its polyline: rejected (2b's path);
- solid: plain (2b's path);
- otherwise: shaped (a flag bit).

Only shaped slots pay, and only explore variants carry the code: the
ribbon's, plus new fan and pluck-still variants. With the mode off, the
programs keep master's text and cost.

Costs:
- Profile writes on growth frames, for a handful of slots. That makes the
  row-partial gap upload (residual above) a prerequisite.
- A fetch and the look function per fragment and shaped slot.
- Register pressure in the explore program. Protect's mere presence costs
  about 0.15 ms, so measure the mode on with no shaped slot.

Alternatives:
- (a) A ctrl per polyline point (about 15 texels), with kExploreAt read
  from KPAINT inside the gap code. Nothing to write on growth frames, but
  KPAINT must be bound in the fan and pluck programs, and every fragment
  pays a VIS scan.
- (b) Clip the window to the shown part, on the CPU only. Cheap, but the
  cut ends square across the under thread instead of following the fade
  and the look.

**The proof.** For each shaped slot, the ideal cut is the geometric cut
times the over thread's drawn coverage at the projection (the CPU mirror
in paint_math). Compare it with the rendered cut: the frame minus the
frame with the slot rejected.
- Fail on a ghost pixel (a cut where the coverage is 0) or an under-cut
  one (where it is 1), and report the error in between.
- The sweep:
  - step `reach` so fronts cross chosen crossings in 0.05 ctrl steps

  - pin growth from 0.1 to 0.9;
  - every look;
