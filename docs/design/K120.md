# K120: faster, smoother input (arrow keys, and a quick phone control)

Levi (2026-10-13, LEDGER K120): hold an arrow key to keep advancing,
taking the branch that matches the key at each junction it reaches; a
quick control on phones too, such as screen-region buttons. No codec
params. One Opus agent, one stage, two parts.

Today the maze has no keyboard play at all: a move is a tap or click on a
run at the head's junction (`handleFabricTap` in `src/main.ts`, about
line 2476: `fabric.edgeAt`, then `fabricController.tapEdge` and
`fabric.beginMove`). K80's real-time mode sends taps through
`handleChaseTap` (about line 2320) instead: a tap while moving QUEUES (the
latest wins) and leaves on arrival, and the chase replays from its inputs.

## Part A: the arrow keys

1. A press of an arrow key moves along the choice whose screen direction
   best matches the key, as a tap on that run would: advance, backtrack
   (the run you came from, when it matches), or win. In real time it goes
   through the chase's queue, like a tap.
2. Holding the key keeps advancing: at each junction the dot reaches, it
   takes the choice that matches the key, until none matches. Decide and
   say: the matching rule (the angle tolerance, and how a run's direction
   is read: its tangent at the head, or a chord some distance along, in
   screen space through the camera), what a hold does when two choices
   match about equally, that a hold never turns straight back on the run
   it just arrived on (or it ping-pongs), and where it stops (a dead-end
   tip, the finish, no match). Measure the thresholds on real layouts (a
   census of junctions over at least 20 settled mazes, in the page or the
   vite-node harness that reproduces the browser's physics; physics.md),
   don't guess them.
3. When a held key fires its next move: on the dot's arrival, so the
   player sees each junction (a tap mid-animation already works; check
   what it does). Ignore the browser's own key repeat (`e.repeat`).
4. Your calls, said in the report: diagonals (two arrows at once), WASD,
   showing which run a held key will take (the choice hover,
   `fabric.setHover`), and anything else that makes it feel quick.
5. Keys must not steal input: the open panel (Tweakpane rows use the
   arrows), the stats modal (`src/ui/daily_stats_view.ts` has its own
   keydown), text fields, modifier chords, knot mode (no maze), the intro
   before the maze is playable, and a solved maze.
6. Everything a tap records, a key move records too: the move log, the
   daily's saved attempt (K47: an attempt IS its tap list, so a reload
   resumes it and the replay agrees), stats, stars (K155), the share
   grid, and the chase's replay inputs.
