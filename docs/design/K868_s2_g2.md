## Why

Levi chose the border look: two edges per thread, a hollow interior and no
gaps. He asked to see curvature-matched (G2) corners next to the G1
rolling-ball arc ("Prototype curvature match too"). An arc meets an arm
with a curvature jump (from the side curve's curvature to 1/r), which the
eye can read as a kink at the tangent point; a G2 corner removes the jump.
S1b also found that the depth rule fails at k42's cyclic triple (finding
1), and that 59 of muse_hash's 342 corner solves fail (finding 3).

## Order (commit after each)

1. **Depth from the diagram** (§3.3). Compute z per chain from the scene's
   crossing table, not from its z samples (physics zPos):
   - pinned at each crossing's arc length on each chain: +depthAmp on the
     over pass, −depthAmp on the under;
   - blended between a chain's crossings, continuously;
   - the physics tent where a crossing is isolated (no other crossing on
     that chain within 2·depthWindow).
   Keep the old rule selectable for comparison. Record your blend in §3.3
   (in the worktree).
   Add a check (the next free number in `checks.ts`): every crossing keeps
   its recorded over/under at its centre, on every synthetic scene and on
   muse_hash. k42 must pass #6 and #7 outside the area its three tubes
   share; its report gives that area's size and which top wins there.
2. **The corner solve.** Root-cause the 59 failed q solves on muse_hash
   ("q solve residual", `.claude/probes/K868/oracle/muse_hash/b.json`).
   Fix them, or say why a given corner has no ball, and add a test. Every
   corner variant below starts from V, q, t_A and t_B, so this comes
   first.
3. **G2 corners** (§3.7). Build these candidates as exact curves in the
   oracle, selectable per render:
   - a clothoid pair: curvature linear in arc length, from the side
     curve's to a peak and back. This is the reference for
     "curvature-matched";
   - a G2 Bézier (cubic or quintic) whose end curvature matches the side
     curve's;
   - an arc spline (a few arcs of graded radius; its distance is closed
     form on a GPU), with its curvature steps reported;
   - the superelliptic arc, if time allows.
   On curved arms, match the side curve's curvature at the join, not 0;
   on polyline arms, estimate that curvature over a window and say how.
   Size each candidate two ways against the G1 arc of the corner's r:
   - the same apex inset: the distance from V to the curve along the
     bisector. This is the main comparison, since it reads as the same
     roundness;
   - the same tangent points t_A and t_B, so the web cap still holds.
   Record what each candidate does when an arm is short (another junction
   close by) or when the web cap bites.
4. **Combs and sheet E** (`K868=E`). Draw a curvature comb (curvature
   normals along the outline, through the corner and a stretch of each
   arm) for G1 and for each candidate, and give the curvature jump at
   the joins as a number. Sheet E has two parts:
   - rows of synthetic corners: t90, y120, acute30 and acute20 under the
     web cap, and one corner with curved arms. Each row shows G1, then the
     candidates, each at 1x and 6x with its comb;
   - muse_hash's j123 and j49 crops: G1 next to your best G2 at the same
     r.
   Use r from the proposed rule (§3.8: master's mapping, capped at 2h,
   plus the web cap ℓ ≤ 1.5h, `webCap` in `oracle.ts`), since Levi has not
   picked yet. Add a k42 row: the old depth rule against the new one. The
   captions give the size match and the numbers.
5. **For S3**, a table per candidate:
   - the curvature jump;
   - how a fragment shader would evaluate the distance: in closed form,
     by iteration, or baked into per-frame sample tables (with the CPU
     cost per corner);
   - your recommendation.

If time runs short: sheet E with G1, the clothoid pair and the Bézier
matters more than a fourth candidate.
