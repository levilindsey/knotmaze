### Decisions, with reasons

- **The depth blend.** Each crossing pins its pass at +amp (over) or
  -amp (under). Each pin is a hat of half-width min(DEPTH_WINDOW, the
  gap to the neighbouring pin), and the hats sum. It is exact at the
  pins, linear between close pins (no overshoot), the physics' own tent
  where a pin is isolated, and continuous in s and in the pin positions,
  so a sliding crossing cannot pop. The physics' summed tent never
  inverted a centre on the scenes measured (worst 0.93 amp on k42, 1.35
  amp on muse_hash); pinned gives 2 amp of separation at every centre.
- **Clothoid pair.** Two clothoid arcs, curvature linear in arc length
  from side A's curvature at the join to a peak and back to side B's.
  The unknowns (the two lengths and the peak) come from a damped Newton
  on the end point from nine starts; the peak is an output, not a
  choice. It is the reference for "curvature-matched".
- **Bézier: degree 5.** A cubic that matches both end curvatures has no
  free parameter left (its two handle lengths solve two coupled
  quadratics, which may have no solution), so nothing is left to tune
  for one hump; the cubic was not built. The quintic matches each end's
  curvature through its third control point's offset along the normal
  (1.25·k·e²) and leaves the handle e = λ·ℓ free (ℓ: the end's tangent
  length). No single λ works: below a threshold that depends on the
  turn the comb has two humps, above it the peak rises fast (one hump
  from λ 0.38 at a 20° turn, 0.33 at 90°, 0.28 at 120°, under 0.2 at
  150°; probe `s2/lambda.ts`). So λ is picked per corner: the lowest
  peak among one-hump λ in [0.05, 0.6] (a 0.01 grid, refined to 0.001).
  Lowest peak because G1's 1/r is the floor, and the closest peak reads
  closest to G1.
- **Arc spline: three arcs.** End arcs at half the middle arc's
  curvature, each turning a quarter of the corner, so its four
  curvature steps are equal (κ_m/2): the fewest arcs that halve G1's
  jump. Built for straight, symmetric ends only; otherwise it reports
  why not and the corner keeps G1's arc.
- **Side curvature on polyline arms.** A Menger circle through the
  centreline at s - h, s and s + h, offset to the outline: side A gets
  -κ/(1 - hκ), side B κ/(1 + hκ) (travel orientation). One h of window
  smooths the polyline's vertex steps without blurring the arm's own
  bend (curved: R 6h).
- **Matching on curved arms.** Apex inset: both feet move out along
  their arms by one centreline arc length σ (an h/8 march, then
  bisection) until the curve crosses V→q at G1's inset (vq - r). Exact
  on straight arms; within 0.022h on curved polyline arms, where
  inset(σ) jumps as a foot crosses a vertex (the side's tangent turns by
  ds/R), and the closer bracket end is kept. One σ for both arms keeps
  the corner symmetric where G1's is, with one unknown. Tangent points:
  σ = 0, G1's t_A and t_B exactly.
- **Sheet E's r.** t90, y120 and curved use §3.8's r with the web cap.
  acute30 and acute20 use r = h under the cap, so the cap bites
  (r = 1.5h·tan(θ/2)): §3.8 gives 0.035h at acute30 and 0 at acute20
  at the synthetic cr of 3, which would show no corner at all.

How a fragment shader would get the distance:

- **G1 arc** (master's): closed form, |p - q| - r inside the wedge.
- **Clothoid pair**: no closed form. A foot on a clothoid needs the
  Fresnel integrals inside a Newton on arc length, two pieces per
  corner: too much branching and too many flops per fragment. Bake it:
  the CPU builds each corner (the Newton above) and uploads N samples
  per corner; the shader takes the least distance over those segments.
- **Quintic Bézier**: no closed form either; the foot solves a degree-9
  polynomial. Iteration from the 6 control points is possible (Newton
  from 2-3 starts, Horner evaluations), but a baked polyline is cheaper
  per fragment and has no convergence risk. Its chord sag with N
  segments is about L²·κ_peak/(8N²): with L 3h and κ_peak 0.7/h, 16
  segments sag 0.0031h (0.03 px at h = 10 px, 0.16 px at muse_hash's
  h = 51 px), so 16-24 samples per corner hold sub-pixel accuracy.
- **Arc spline**: closed form, three circle distances with sector tests
  (about 3x the G1 arc), from 3 centres, radii and sector bounds per
  corner, cheap to build on straight symmetric corners. But it is not
  G2: its four steps are half G1's jump each, 30-160x the 0.01 target,
  and it builds only on straight symmetric ends (none of muse_hash's
  341); curved or asymmetric arms need a general arc spline with more
  arcs.

**Recommendation: the quintic Bézier, baked, once the muse_hash
failures are attributed.** On the synthetic scenes it has exact G2
joins, one hump and the lowest peak of the three (the closest to G1's
look: 0.70 against G1's 0.67 at t90). Cost: at a fixed λ its
constructor takes 0.07-0.08 ms per corner, so with λ from a table (by
turn and end curvatures) and the inset met in closed form on straight
arms, a bake is about 0.1 ms per corner: about 34 ms if all 341 of
muse_hash's corners moved in one frame. So S3 rebuilds only the corners
whose junction moved (the knot sleeps at rest), or bakes in the physics
worker. The λ search (about 6 ms) and the σ march around it (about
250 ms) are oracle tools, not frame work. The clothoid is the fallback
(0.06-0.13 ms per corner, no λ) at a higher peak (0.90 at t90) and with
a Newton that failed at 47 of muse_hash's corners.
