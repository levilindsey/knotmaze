# K74 brief: what makes a good maze, measured against ours

Levi, 2026-10-12:

> "In general, we need to take a step back and think about good maze
> design. Please do some research. One of the things that comes to mind
> is that there shouldn't be too many junctions near the finish,
> otherwise it's too easy to find a path through by just moving naiively
> in the most direct direction to the end. After understanding
> characteristics that lead to good maze design, I want you to research
> and plan possible ways that we could alter our maze formulation to
> achieve some of these good design characteristics.
>
> A related consideration is our dead ends. They are less stubby, but
> still too stubby in general. We should do something to ensure longer
> dead end paths with no junctions--possibly with a junction-removal pass
> after?"

And later: "it looks like adjusting some of these 'Maze' params do give
maze characteristics that are probably 'better maze design'. But lmk."

## Work

1. **Research.** Cover:
   - maze texture (Pullen's Think Labyrinth; Buck's *Mazes for
     Programmers*);
   - difficulty and complexity studies (McClendon, and solver-based
     metrics);
   - puzzle and level-design writing (decision density, decoys, a
     solution that moves away from the goal, resisting "head for the
     exit");
   - braided mazes versus perfect ones.

   Keep the sources.
2. **Measure our mazes** with a vite-node harness in `.claude/probes/K74/`
   (modeled on K46 and K63): 50+ designs at the defaults and the DAILY_V1
   days, `deadEnds` on, and K46 recipes. Measure:
   - junction density against distance to the exit and the entrance;
   - greedy "head for the exit" walkers (solve rate, cost vs optimal);
   - solution-path decisions and away-from-goal stretches;
   - dead-end junction-free lengths.

   FIRST, sweep the existing knobs: junctionSpacing, exitClearance,
   entranceClearance, branchDensity, fourWayFraction, deadEnds.
3. **Plan formulation changes.** Candidates:
   - exit-region junction thinning;
   - decoys toward the exit;
   - forcing away-from-goal stretches;
   - a junction-removal pass for long dead ends.

   For each, give the mechanism, a measured prototype, risks (planarity,
   alternation, physics) and cost.
   - **Binding:** generation changes move every daily and every link.
     Ship behind appended codec params whose defaults reproduce today's
     maze, or a DAILY_V2 from a future day. Relate each option to K46.

## Facts

- The plan works in DOUBLED lattice units (one cell = 2).
- `clearOf` (maze_plan.ts) keeps choice junctions, 4-way merges and

#### H1, as the design doc measured it

The numbers below were measured on V1, before stage 1 (exit clearance 5,
no dead ends).
- **An H** is a splice: two host threads, a junction on each (A and B)
  and a short bridge between them (`proto_hcut.ts`, header).
- **The cut.** At B, one of its two host arms is cut:
  - B becomes a plain corner (host -> bridge);
  - the cut arm is trimmed back to its first crossing, and that crossing
    is fused into a 3-way junction. This is the trim that cuts already
    use (`MazeCutTrim`, `deleteStub`).
- **What it changes.** Junctions, loops and decisions stay the same.
  Bridge taps per solve drop from 5 of 19 to 1, and ladders from 8 to 0.
- **Safeguards:**
  - options are tried smoothest corner first;
  - same-strand splices and self-crossings are excluded;
  - no crossing on an end's run (the entrance and exit tails, a dead-end
    tail) is fused;
  - the walk graph stays reachable from the start. That check fired on 4
    of 80 mazes with long dead ends.
- **Coverage.** 69 of 93 H's were cut. The rest have a junction or an end
  before any crossing on every arm.
- **Costs.**
  - The greedy walks get a little easier: far-end 1.96x -> 1.84x,
    heading 2.84x -> 2.31x.
  - The last motion comes at 1.2 s instead of 0.6 s, with 6% fewer
    particles. 16 of 16 slept.

At today's defaults the far-end greedy walk is 3.12x in the median, with
11% of dailies within 1.5x (stage 1). Decision 1's bar is at least 2.5x,
with at most 15% within 1.5x. H1 may cost some of that margin, so measure
it before picking the default.

**What landed**
- **H1, the fused arm cut.** `fuseHCuts` in `src/core/maze_apply.ts` (its
  header comment says the rules) runs last in `applyMazeCuts` when the
  plan carries `hCutShare` above 0, and returns its counts (`HCutStats`:
  bridges, attempted, cut, skipped, rejected, whole); otherwise it returns
  null.
  - **The cut.** For an H's junction B, one of B's host arms is removed
    back to its first crossing X, X is fused into a 3-way junction (the
    trims' surgery, done inline rather than through `deleteStub`), and B
    becomes a turn: the thread runs host -> B -> bridge -> A.
  - **Which H's.** `round(share x bridges)` splice bridges, at an even
    stride in strand order (the prototype's). Within an H, both ends as B
    and both arms of each are options, tried smoothest kept arm first.
  - **Never:** an arm that meets a junction or an end before a crossing; a
    crossing on an END's run up to its first junction (the entrance, the
    exit, the long tails; the set is refreshed after each cut); a
    self-crossing of the host; a splice whose two ends sit on one strand.
  - **Reachability.** Each option is checked before any surgery, by a
    union-find over the walk vertices of the strands it would produce
    (exactly the walk graph's components); an option that splits the walk
    is refused and the next is tried. This replaces the prototype's
    snapshot, rebuild and restore with the same guarantee.
  - When the kept strand runs B to A, the bridge's edge is turned around
    (nodes swapped, points reversed), so edges stay in traversal order.
