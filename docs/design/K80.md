# K80 brief: the minotaur, and other gameplay additions

Levi, 2026-10-12 (verbatim):

> "Ooh, I just thought of a fun optional mode I want to add--the
> minotaur. If you touch it, you lose. It also moves through the
> maze--one segment each turn you move. We should show a red highlight
> behind its position. It should start at the finish. We should be a bit
> thoughtful about what sort of a movement pattern will feel good for it.
> Maybe somewhat random at the start, until the player gets close, then
> it approaches/follows the player? Or maybe it paces back and forth? And
> would a random path choose random destinations within a radius, travel
> there over however many turns, then choose another (that's probably
> better than a purely random choice of all adjacent segments each turn).
> And also, if we do add a chase behavior, would that always choose the
> optimal segment? Would it make mistakes, or stall in-place some turns?
> And which of these aspects should be controllable with parameters?  Do
> some research, and propose design options and trade/offs.
>
> Also, in general, I wonder about other possible gameplay additions we
> could add. Like locked segments, and keys that could be collected to get
> through locks. Or one-way segments. Or segments that are destroyed after
> you cross them. Or little pickups/critters you have to collect before
> getting to the end (or that act as optional bonuses when you get to the
> end). Or a sword you can get that lets you kill the minotaur. Or ether
> enemies/obstacles that move in predictable patterns, but you have to
> watch, pay attention, and remember to be able to anticipate their next
> movement (in which case, particular care would need to be paid to how
> and where we place them within the maze). Please research options like
> these and other possible gameplay additions or considerations. Please
> give me a long list of possible additions and their trade-offs."

## Our medium (read first)

- The maze is a woven knot, and the threads ARE the paths: CLAUDE.md,
  `.claude/rules/gameplay.md` and `generation.md`.
- A player move is one tap at a branch point, running ONE thread run
  from junction to junction (`src/game/fabric_controller.ts`). That is
  the natural turn for "one segment per move".
- Backtracking is allowed. Trail mode forbids it. Mazes are braided
  (loops) by default; long dead ends exist with `deadEnds` on.
- The walk graph (`src/core/walk_graph.ts`) carries run lengths and
  Dijkstra.
- Existing modes to build on: fog of war, the pathViz modes, the K76
  thread-reveal mode (design in flight).
- The daily maze (K20, K46's variety proposal with a Thursday modifier
  slot): a mode can be a daily modifier.
- Stats and the Wordle-style share grid (`game/maze_stats.ts`,
  `share_text.ts`).
- Constraints: links are complete state (new params are appended to the
  codec with old-behavior defaults); generation changes move every daily
  and link; dailies are pinned by versioned definitions; phones are a
  first-class target.

## Research

Use primary sources where you can:
- Robert Abbott's "Theseus and the Minotaur" and his logic mazes
  (one-way paths, multi-state rules);
- Pac-Man's ghost AI (scatter / chase / frightened, per-ghost targeting);
- turn-based patrol puzzles (Hitman GO, Lara Croft GO, Crypt of the
  NecroDancer, Into the Breach's telegraphing);
- roguelike monster AI (wander, hunt, scent and flow maps, e.g. Brogue);
- lock-and-key generation: Dormans' mission graphs, cyclic dungeon
  generation (Unexplored), Boris the Brave's lock-and-key articles, Mark
  Brown's "Boss Keys";
- fairness and solvability guarantees for chase games on graphs (cop and
  robber, pursuit-evasion), including how loops change it.

## Deliverable

`docs/design/K80_gameplay_additions.md`, recommendations and Levi's
questions first.

1. **Minotaur design options.** Cover:
   - movement models: purpose-driven wandering (destinations within a
     radius), patrols and pacing, proximity-triggered chase, Abbott's
     deterministic rule, Pac-Man-style modes;
   - chase quality (optimal, noisy, stalling, a memory of where you were
     last seen) and speed (runs per move, sub-run positions);
   - its start at the finish and luring;
   - fairness: no forced loss on spawn, no unwinnable traps, guarantees
     on braided versus dead-end mazes;
   - telegraphing: showing its next move;
   - the red highlight and lose and retry UX;
   - stats and share;
   - interaction with backtracking, fog, the reveal mode and dailies.

   For each option give the trade-offs and which parts should be params
   (names, ranges, defaults).
2. **A long list of other additions,** each with:
   - what it is and why it is fun;
   - how it fits our medium (runs, junctions, threads, rendering, plucks
     and sound);
   - generation and solvability needs (placement algorithms, for example
     lock-and-key placement on the walk graph);
   - UX on phones;
   - daily and share implications;
   - cost and risk.

   Include Levi's ideas (locks and keys, one-way runs, collapsing runs,
   required and bonus pickups, a sword, patterned enemies with careful
   placement) and many more. Think of the knot as an instrument too:
   musical locks and plucks as actions.
3. **A suggested order:** what makes a good first mode, what composes,
   what to avoid.

No code. Light prototypes or simulations of AI behaviors in
`.claude/probes/K80/` are welcome when they settle a question (for
example, how often a given chase rule traps the player on our walk
graphs).
