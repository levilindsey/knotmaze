Ledger: K580 (its open calls: K606). Parent: K115, cursive text. One

## Levi's call (2026-10-20, the report card)

Levi's note, verbatim:

> Go with my tangent request. It's specifically ok for the tight angles of
> these joins, and that is essential for it to actually look like cursive
> text. I also want to explicitly say that the spur formed in cases like
> this should have some taper, which makes them nonstandard from other
> non-letter junctions. The taper should show the dead-end segment leaving
> the junction wider than normal (but at a width that would match the
> continuous angles of both the entering and leaving storkes that are being
> merged into the one spur segment) and this spur then tapers to a normal
> width whenever the strokes should become collinear.

The coordinator's reading. Check it against the real geometry and say
where it does not fit:
- **The join.** The a keeps the tangent o-kind join (`A_GLYPH`, already
  the default). `A_LANDED` stays only as a probe comparison.
- **The taper.** Where the connector and the oval converge onto one path,
  the stretch where they overlap only partly is drawn as ONE ribbon, and
  that ribbon belongs to the spur.
  - At the junction it is as wide as the two strokes side by side: its
    edges continue the outer edges of the connector and of the oval
    without a kink ("continuous angles").
  - It narrows to the normal width where the two strokes become collinear
    (today's J), and runs at normal width from there to the tip S.
  - So the junction moves back along the strokes to where they part. A
    solid tapered root replaces the thorn, the 1-degree sliver of two
    overlapping ribbons.
- **Where it applies.** Only letter joins declared tangent get the taper;
  every other junction draws as today. Make it a property of the spur
  junction in the glyph data, so c, d, g, o and q can reuse it when they
  are authored.
- **The lint.** A tangent join waives the fork rule (15 degrees, 1.2 LU)
  and the clearance floor inside its taper span, and checks the taper
  instead (step 3). Ordinary forks keep the fork rule; that is the
  coordinator's reading of Q2, and K606 asks Levi to confirm it.
- **The wider i and a** (Q3, K606) stay as they are. Build nothing that
  depends on that answer.

## Steps

1. **Geometry first, on the authored cubics.** Work out three things and
   pick each by stills at xh 2 and 3:
   - where the two strokes part. Candidates: where the ribbons' inner
     edges meet, or where the gap between them reaches the clearance
     floor;
   - the root width there;
   - the taper's profile from there to collinear: following the two
     strokes' outer edges exactly, or a smooth monotone fit.
2. **Carry the taper through every consumer of a thread's width,** behind
   `#glyphs2` only. A consumer you miss draws a mismatched edge.
   - the ribbon mesh and its casing;
   - the crossing gaps and the gap search's tables (RADII in
     `src/render/samp_table.ts`, if that is where width lives);
   - the junction fans and the arm strips;
   - the reveal's width masks, fog, and the thread reveal;
   - hit tests;
   - physics collision, or show that it needs nothing.
3. **The lint.** Declare the join tangent in the data. For a tangent join,
   replace the fork and clearance checks inside the taper span with taper
   checks:
   - both edges are G1 with the strokes' outer edges;
   - the width falls monotonically from the junction to the collinear
     point, and is normal width there;
   - the junction's crotch leaves no background sliver thinner than the
     clearance floor, or a bound you measure.

   The tangent a moves from known failure to pass at xh 2 and 3.
4. **Contact sheets for Levi.** Show the a alone, "ai", "ia" and "aa"
   (whatever the set builds):
   - at xh 2 and 3, seeds 7 and 9;
   - at desktop scale and at the Pixel 11's scale;
   - comparing the tapered tangent (the new default), stage 9's untapered
     tangent and `A_LANDED`.

   Attribute the "ai" notch if it is cheap; if it is the junction render,
   say so.
5. **Tests.**
   - the lint tests, plus a unit test for the taper's width profile;
   - the knot golden stays byte-identical;
   - nothing changes with `#glyphs2` off: titles still draw in Hershey;
   - the suite and the build pass.

If the cap comes first, stop with the most useful subset, in this order:
- the geometry and prototype stills (steps 1 and 4);
- then the consumers (step 2);
- then the lint (step 3).
