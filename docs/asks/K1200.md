## Question
The light now runs around loops. When a loop leaves your position and comes back to it, pulses enter it from both ends at once and meet halfway round, where they fade out in pairs. The old light ran two trains across each other there, with hard edges (the top row of the picture). Because the new pulses vanish in pairs where they meet, the far side of a long loop goes dark for a moment, a stretch about twice the usual gap between pulses (bottom row, the second and third moments). Is that fine, or should the pulses pass through each other and carry on around the loop?

## Context
- The dark stretch varies with the loop: on one test loop it was barely there, on the two longer ones about twice the usual gap.
- Your earlier answers stand: the light splits at a fork and runs toward you behind you. Going straight through your own junction without a dim moment is the next stage, separate from this question.

## Options
- **Fine as is (rec)**: pulses meet halfway and fade out in pairs; the dark stretch where they meet stays.
- **Pass through each other**: each pulse carries on past the halfway point, round to where the loop comes back to you, so the two trains cross on the far side with no extra dark stretch (like the old light, without its hard edges).
- **Something else**: say what you see and what you want.
